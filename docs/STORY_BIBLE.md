# EchoStory Story Bible

## Core premise

One ordinary night in Coeur d’Alene, reality slips at midnight.

The protagonist crosses into a version of CDA that is almost right but unmistakably wrong. A doorway back to his old life appears almost immediately. He refuses it.

That choice becomes the central act of the story.

What begins as an alternate-reality mystery gradually becomes personal: this world seems to know him, an older version of his own voice guides him through a truck radio, objects and memories move between realities, and evidence suggests the life he called “normal” may have been the displacement.

The deeper question is not simply **How does he get home?**

It becomes:

**Which world was home in the first place — and which version of him made that decision before?**

---

# Three-act arc

## Act I — The Crossing | Tracks 1–3

The protagonist moves from ordinary life into displacement. The mystery remains mostly physical and external: clocks, roads, radio signals, people, geography.

By the end of Act I he knows:
1. This is not simply the future.
2. Someone here already knows him.
3. The voice guiding him is his own.

**Emotional movement:** comfort → unease → fear → curiosity

## Act II — The Fracture | Tracks 4–7

The mystery stops being mainly about the world and becomes about him. Reflections fail. Another version of him appears. The lake behaves like a memory boundary. Older-him reveals rules. Déjà vu becomes impossible memory.

By the end of Act II he can no longer assume this is his first crossing.

**Emotional movement:** curiosity → pursuit → dread → recognition

## Act III — The Return | Tracks 8–10

The original premise turns inside out. The strange world may actually be his origin. The doorway was never primarily an escape route. Older-him has withheld part of the truth. The protagonist must decide whether to repeat the cycle, break it, or accept a new role inside it.

**Emotional movement:** recognition → betrayal → choice → acceptance

# Track canon

## 1. The Last Normal Night — The Crossing

**Opening state:** Relaxed, grounded, mildly restless. An ordinary CDA night that feels unimportant.

**Anchor details:** Rainier sign, Sherman lights, lake after dark, truck, phone battery.

**Required markers:**
- Ordinary Coeur d’Alene night.
- Small anomalies are easy to dismiss.
- Clocks converge on **11:59**.
- Midnight causes the first rupture.
- He wakes in a recognizable but altered reality.
- A doorway/view into his old world appears.
- He consciously refuses to go back.
- His phone returns to service with one message: **WELCOME HOME**.

**Discovery:** Reality can split.

**Decision:** He stays.

**Consequence:** The new world now has access to him.

**Recurring signal:** First full wooden-flute growl during the rupture.

**Question created:** Why did this world say **WELCOME HOME**?

**Handoff:** Morning arrives from the wrong side.

---

## 2. The Wrong Side of Morning — Exploration

**Opening state:** Alert but not panicked. He expects daylight to make the situation understandable.

**Anchor details:** Morning lake, Sherman Avenue, truck, road signs, phone map.

**Required markers:**
- Sunrise appears subtly wrong.
- Familiar streets connect incorrectly.
- A road or landmark he knows is missing.
- A building exists where one should not.
- Lake markers/navigation references do not match his memory.
- At least two strangers recognize him.
- His phone map disagrees with his memory of CDA.
- He finds hard proof this is **not simply the future**.
- Truck radio crackles to life on **Frequency Three**.

**Discovery:** This is a different version of the place, not merely a later date.

**Decision:** He stops explaining the world as a normal timeline.

**Consequence:** He becomes willing to listen to the impossible.

**Question answered:** Is this just future CDA? **No.**

**Question created:** Why do people here recognize him?

**Handoff:** Through static, a voice says something only he would say.

---

## 3. Frequency Three — The Voice

**Opening state:** Suspicious, fascinated, testing the signal rather than trusting it.

**Anchor details:** Truck dashboard, radio dial, static, repeating mile markers.

**Required markers:**
- Frequency Three activates without being selected.
- The voice sounds older but unmistakably like him.
- The voice predicts a small event seconds before it happens.
- A second warning arrives **after** the event it was meant to prevent.
- He tests the voice with information only he should know.
- The answer proves the speaker is connected to him.
- He realizes transmissions do not move cleanly forward through time.
- Older-him gives one incomplete warning: **Do not follow the man with your shadow.**

**Discovery:** The voice is an older version of himself — or something with access to his memories.

**Decision:** He keeps the radio tuned to Frequency Three.

**Consequence:** Older-him can now influence his choices.

**Question answered:** Who is speaking on the radio? **Older-him.**

**Question created:** Why is older-him afraid of another version of him?

**Handoff:** In the rearview mirror, his reflection turns its head before he does.

---

## 4. The Man in My Shadow — The Doppelgänger

**Opening state:** Hyper-aware. He checks reflections and shadows compulsively.

**Anchor details:** Rearview mirror, storefront glass, streetlights, white car.

**Required markers:**
- Reflections lag by fractions of a second.
- His shadow occasionally points the wrong direction.
- The same white car from Track 1 appears again.
- He sees another version of himself crossing a street.
- The double sees him and clearly recognizes him.
- The protagonist follows.
- The double runs.
- During the chase, the double drops an object tied unmistakably to the protagonist’s old life.

**Flexible continuity object:** keychain, lighter, coin, photo, or another object he still currently possesses.

**Discovery:** More than one version of him exists here physically.

**Decision:** He keeps the impossible duplicate object.

**Consequence:** He has proof that matter can cross between realities.

**Question answered:** Is the double real? **Yes.**

**Question created:** How can the double possess something he has not lost?

**Handoff:** The duplicate object is wet with lake water.

---

## 5. The Lake Remembers — The Constant

**Opening state:** Drawn toward the lake because of the wet object.

**Anchor details:** Shoreline, docks, underwater sound, lake lights.

**Required markers:**
- The lake occupies the same fundamental place across realities even when the city changes.
- The duplicate object reacts to or leads him toward the shoreline.
- Something from another timeline surfaces.
- Sounds beneath the water resemble distorted memories or conversations.
- He sees or hears a moment from a life he does not remember living.
- He finds something at the shoreline that he **has not lost yet**.
- The wooden-flute growl is heard physically from beneath the lake.

**Discovery:** The lake is a cross-reality constant and memory boundary.

**Decision:** He takes the newly surfaced object.

**Consequence:** Memory bleed begins.

**Question answered:** Can matter cross realities? **Yes.**

**Question created:** Why does the lake contain memories of lives he never lived?

**Handoff:** Frequency Three activates: **You went too close to the water.**

---

## 6. Don’t Answer Yourself — The Rules

**Opening state:** Angry. He demands real answers from older-him.

**Anchor details:** Radio, lake behind him, dark road, dashboard clock.

**Required markers:**
- First sustained two-way conversation with older-him.
- Older-him admits there are known rules.
- Rule 1: never tell another version what year you came from.
- Rule 2: never cross the lake after midnight.
- Rule 3: if your own voice calls your name from behind you, do not turn around.
- Older-him refuses to explain who established the rules.
- The protagonist challenges whether older-him can be trusted.
- He breaks or nearly breaks one rule.
- His own voice calls his name from directly behind him.
- He does **not** immediately turn around.

**Discovery:** Others survived this phenomenon long enough to establish repeatable rules.

**Decision:** He follows Rule 3 despite distrusting its source.

**Consequence:** Whatever is behind him knows he can hear it.

**Question answered:** Are there repeatable laws? **Yes.**

**Question created:** Who learned them first, and what happens when one breaks?

**Handoff:** The voice behind him whispers a childhood memory he does not remember.

---

## 7. I Already Did This — The Fracture

**Opening state:** Memory confusion. He is no longer sure which experiences belong to him.

**Anchor details:** Contradictory familiar locations, duplicate object, childhood phrase.

**Required markers:**
- Déjà vu becomes complete memory.
- He remembers entering buildings he has never visited.
- He remembers conversations that have not happened.
- Multiple versions of one event overlap.
- He recognizes the childhood phrase from Track 6.
- He discovers evidence intentionally left by a previous version of himself.
- The evidence proves he has crossed before — or a version of him expected this exact sequence.
- Previous-him directs him toward a place/person connected to **WELCOME BACK**.

**Discovery:** The crossing is not a unique accident. His identity is entangled with a repeating pattern.

**Decision:** He follows the message left by previous-him.

**Consequence:** He stops merely surviving and begins investigating his own origin.

**Question answered:** Why the impossible memories? **Previous crossings / memory bleed.**

**Question created:** Which version of him was first?

**Handoff:** He reaches a place that recognizes him before he enters.

---

## 8. Welcome Back — The Reveal

**Opening state:** Expecting another trap but vulnerable to anything that feels familiar.

**Anchor details:** Childhood place, old photograph/record, identifying mark or name.

**Required markers:**
- A location, system, person, or archive recognizes him.
- Physical evidence ties him to this world before the life he remembers.
- A childhood memory returns with sensory detail.
- He remembers the lake/world before his remembered “normal” life.
- Older-him confirms part of the truth but still withholds something.
- **WELCOME HOME / WELCOME BACK** is recontextualized as recognition, not greeting.
- The protagonist realizes the “normal” CDA he came from may have been the alternate life.

**Discovery:** This reality may be his original home.

**Decision:** He stops defining his old world as automatically more real.

**Consequence:** Returning through the door is no longer obviously correct.

**Question answered:** Why did the world say WELCOME HOME? **Because it already knew him.**

**Question created:** If this was home, why was he displaced?

**Handoff:** A doorway to the old world opens again, but something moves inside it before he approaches.

---

## 9. The Door Was Never for Me — The Confrontation

**Opening state:** Torn between two identities and two versions of home.

**Anchor details:** Doorway, old street beyond it, truck/radio, two versions of lake ambience.

**Required markers:**
- The original doorway returns.
- He initially assumes he has finally been given a choice to leave.
- Older-him urgently tells him not to approach.
- He notices movement coming **toward** him from the old side.
- He discovers the doorway is not primarily an exit.
- His refusal in Track 1 accidentally prevented something from crossing.
- Older-him admits he knew this would happen.
- The protagonist realizes he was being maneuvered, not merely protected.
- He must choose whether to seal the door, enter it, or use it deliberately.

**Discovery:** The doorway was opened from the other side.

**Decision:** He refuses both the obvious escape and older-him’s instructions. He chooses a third option: control the crossing himself.

**Consequence:** He forces older-him to appear physically.

**Question answered:** Was the doorway an escape? **No. It was ingress.**

**Question created:** What has older-him actually been trying to accomplish?

**Handoff:** Radio goes silent. A man behind him says: **You always choose the third option.** He turns. It is him.

---

## 10. Midnight Knows My Name — The Choice

**Opening state:** Face-to-face with older-him for the first time.

**Anchor details:** Doorway, lake, duplicate objects, silent truck radio, midnight clock.

**Required markers:**
- Older-him appears physically.
- He is not identical to the radio persona the protagonist imagined.
- Older-him reveals competing versions of the same plan/history.
- The first crossing is recontextualized as part accident, part intervention.
- Older-him is not necessarily the oldest version.
- The protagonist realizes endlessly choosing between two realities perpetuates the fracture.
- He rejects repeating older-him’s exact path.
- He seals or stabilizes the immediate breach while accepting there may be no single “correct” timeline.
- He chooses who he will be rather than which reality owns him.
- The local arc resolves.

**Discovery:** There may be no single original version left — only a chain of choices and inherited memories.

**Decision:** Break the repetition instead of recovering a perfect past.

**Consequence:** The current breach stabilizes, but the larger phenomenon survives.

**Question answered:** What did older-him want? **To engineer the choice he once made and preserve his version of the outcome.**

**Final larger question:** How many versions have done this before?

### Final scene

Somewhere in the CDA he left behind:

11:59.

A familiar truck radio turns itself on.

Frequency Three.

Another version of him opens his eyes.

An older voice says:

**You finally made it.**

[wooden-flute growl]

# Chapter structure for every song

Every track should contain these functions even when they are not explicitly labeled in Suno lyrics:

1. **Opening State** — physical and emotional starting point.
2. **Anchor Detail** — familiar object, location, or sound.
3. **Anomaly** — new impossible element introduced in this chapter.
4. **Discovery** — one meaningful answer.
5. **Decision** — the protagonist actively chooses something.
6. **Consequence** — the choice changes the situation.
7. **Recurring Signal** — use only 2–4 continuity motifs.
8. **Handoff** — final image, line, sound, or reveal triggers the next track.
